This is a review and detailed measurements of the Topping M62 Portable (battery operated) Audio Interface (ADC/DAC) and headphone amplifier. It was sent to me by the company and costs US $ 249.
There is a ton of functionality for which, I am not qualified to fully describe. But we are talking about a bunch of inputs and outputs including no less than three USB-C!
Inclusion of such features as OTG and company description implies portable, in-field recording. To that end, a number of signal processing features such as programmable reverb, noise reduction, and limiter are provided. Accompanied software makes operation of the unit much easier. As shipped, it is in "mobile" or should I say, novice mode. It limits sample rate to just 48 kHz. There is a Pro mode where it unlocks all the other rates and importantly, gives you 6-band 10-band PEQ. Alas, I could not find any documentation on how to push it into Pro mode other than a post by a member here. The process includes multiple keystroke/power cycle sequences which I must have gotten wrong. I wish the control software allowed the switch. Or something much simpler on the hardware.
The M62 comes in many colors and there are a lot accessories for it such as a case and such -- clearly aimed at the younger content maker.
The metal enclosure is solid and has enough weight to it to hold on to cables to some extent while not being too heavy or big to carry.

Conclusions 
Topping's entry into professional audio interfaces was just competent. We all expected more but the price was right.
With M62, we see the company producing far better performance in a battery operated portable no less! As such, I am surprised at the positioning toward average content creator. It is far more capable than that with extensive connectivity. And you have a nice headphone jack to boot. Charge the unit and you can enjoy many hours of music listening with an appropriate source.
All of this comes at such a reasonable price.
I am happy to recommend Topping M62 Professional Interface.

Next-Gen Portable Audio Interface – Designed for outdoor/indoor live streaming, podcasts, music recording, and field recording, offering professional-grade sound in a compact device.
Dual Mic Inputs with 48V Phantom Power – Connect and power two condenser or dynamic microphones simultaneously, ideal for interviews, podcasts, and vocal + instrument recording.
Ultra-Linear Preamp & High Dynamic Range – Gain up to 88dB with EIN as low as -129.5dBu, ensuring ultra-low noise and clear, natural sound for professional creators.
Smart Audio Processing – AI noise reduction, noise gate, auto gain, and 3 reverb modes (Studio/Live/Hall) let you optimize vocals with one touch.
Flexible Connectivity – Supports smartphone OTG, Bluetooth (aptX-HD/aptX-LL/AAC/SBC), AUX, and PC/Mac via USB-C, with lossless transmission and multi-source mixing.
Real-Time EQ & Monitoring – Input EQ enhances source recordings, Output EQ customizes listening, with zero-latency monitoring for accurate sound control.
Long Battery Life – 5000mAh battery powers up to 8.5 hours (dynamic mic) or 6.5 hours (dual condenser mics), perfect for extended live sessions.
Powerful Headphone Output – 500mW x2 @32Ω with ultra-low 1Ω output impedance easily drives up to 600Ω headphones for precise monitoring.

A critical part of a product's success as a digital recording interface is its control software and ease of use. Is this product meant for use in a portable recording setup? How exactly is that meant to be set up? Does this device need to be connected to a laptop or smartphone to record audio? Or can it be used standalone with a USB storage drive connected?
daniboun just mentioned that there are no high impedance instrument inputs. That means if you wanted to record an electric guitar straight into this device, you'd need some kind of a preamp/buffer (a 'guitar preamp' with gain , not just an electric guitar effects pedal or a unity-gain buffer).
The product also lacks XLR inputs. That's a shame. But it does have 48V DC phantom power. That's good. I guess recordists will need to factor in the cost of acquiring XLR to TRS adapters for their existing mic cables, as microphones require XLR connectors on their end.
Another critical consideration is how well this device gets along with the dominant DAW apps out there. Does its driver play well with Pro Tools, Cubase, Audacity, Reaper, Ableton Live, GarageBand, Logic, etc?
I see there's a digital reverb included in the device. How does that sound? Is it competitive with the VST plugins available (many of them cheap or free)? If the reverb is usable then that's a plus, but that's an unnecessary feature unless the built in reverb algorithms provide a lot of control and are truly exceptional sounding. I guess adding a digital reverb is cheap and easy to do, so why not? Hopefully it's a usable feature and not just a distraction.
In addition, the product includes built in PEQ. Again, that's a feature that's covered in all the popular DAW apps. What is the intended use for this device? Is it expected that the PEQ will be used during live recording ('tracking'), instead of in the post-tracking/mixing phase? 
Also, what would the Bluetooth interface be used for, in the real world? Is that both input and output, as in the designers expect users to be able to record from Bluetooth headsets? For musicians (and to me), that looks like another unnecessary feature that was cheap and easy to add on.
I don't see separate volume controls for studio monitor loudspeakers and headphones. Are those separately adjustable from this device? Or is this meant to be used with headphone monitoring only?
The ADC measurements are truly impressive, but it looks like it might lack some of the features people expect to find in a musician's home studio or portable live music recording interface. I could be wrong, though.
Necessities of a home recording studio type of AD/DA interface:
1. Balanced XLR inputs for microphones
2. 1/4" inputs for either balanced line level sources or unbalanced electric guitar/bass
3. +48V DC phantom power for condenser microphones
4. High impedance instrument input mode for recording electric guitar, bass guitar straight to the interface.
5. Monitor level control (for controlling your studio monitor loudspeaker setup separately from the line out volume level adjustment).
6. Headphone out with its own level control.
7. A good, easy-to-use app or hardware interface to switch on/off features and settings (Hi-Z/Lo-Z, phantom power, active inputs/outputs, bit depth/sample rate, etc.)
Measurements are one thing, but this device should be a tool to get something concrete done in the real world. Can it do it gracefully, with ease and flexibility? Or is this all about the glamor of the best possible measurements, with usability features compromised?
Again, I could be wrong about all of this. If I am, please clarify so I and others may know what this device can and cannot do as a musician's recording interface. Thanks.

As a note, it is 10-band PEQ, not 6 
I've owned the M62 for a while now - I actually sold my RME UFX III and Babyface Pro FS and UCX II and RME 2/4 and Neumann MT48 and just have this and the Topping DX1 II now which is admittedly sort of odd, but as I no longer have a surround setup: I really only need two i/o and this combo has been working fine with a few annoyances. Overall for recording: having 2 nice preamps and 2 outputs for $250 is pretty amazing, so any notes below I have is being pretty picky considering what all you get with the M62
Some M62 Notes:
Size is about two phones stacked on top of each other. Smaller/Lighter than Babyface Pro FS.
Drivers so far have had no issues at all in Pro Tools on MAC/PC. I don't recall them being any better or worse so far than RME/MOTU/Neumann drivers actually. 
10 Band EQ! MOTU doesn't have this and only the highest end RME interfaces has this many ... not even the new ADI EX has 10 band EQ (which is crazy in 2026 that a device that expensive doesn't have 10 band EQ and not any better converters than the old model)
Lack of Guitar DI is frustrating, but I picked up a Triton Bigamp in-line Guitar DI and it works fine.
Max input levels are fine for my mics, but if I was recording drums: might need an external pad: so a pad or padless preamps with higher input level may be beneficial in future desktop models (like THAT 1580 preamps)
Latency is about as good if not better than Neumann MT48, but not as good as MOTU/RME.
The stepped gain for volume output and input is very wanted! That's why I am okay with this whereas I sold the Topping E8x8 and didn't pick up the other smaller topping interfaces as you can't match stereo mic pres with those, nor can you set an exact value for output. (8x8 doesn't really work as a surround interface which is a shame)
The low latency mixing in the M Control software in there is fine though - for tracking/etc - that's all that's really needed.
The remote control is great: I wish all studio interfaces had a remote control!
The colored LED's can be a bit confusing for levels of headphone output vs a normal screen like on the DX1 II that says -25/etc.
The buttons/combo button presses/color the circle is and how to change modes can be confusing.
The lack of digital I/O is disappointing: if it had optical out you could easily plug it into a DX1 II and have a great combo.
OTG is confusing/not really a pro standard. Digital I/O would have been preferred at least for my use-case/most any professional.
I can't actually figure out how to get this to record on my phone easily. If they could make a way to plug in a USB stick to the OTG port to record - it would make this 100x better, or a way to plug a DAC into it for two extra outputs would also make this 100x better.
I do agree it's a strange use-case device where it's lacking some things for a dedicated desktop interface like digital i/o or a second set of analog outs so you can hook up speakers, but as a portable device: it doesn't have a way to record direct to the device for just on the go recording random sounds/etc and is probably a bit too complicated for most casual users who probably don't care about 114 SINAD inputs. Even pro portable recorders like a Mixpre or zoom recorder: usually you just stick in a USB stick and press the big red record button and that's all.
I can see why there are no XLR outputs and you need a TRS to XLR adaptor - it's too thin. At least it's a normal TRS to XLR adaptor that's needed unlike the DX1 II or E1DA gear where you may need more rare stereo balanced type cable adaptors.
RME Babyface PRO FS is probably closest to this - both are fine: more options for software routing on the Babyface, but the M62 has 10 bands of EQ which the RME doesn't have and the AD/DA conversion is much better on the M62 and it's also smaller and battery powered when required and USB-C is nowadays is smarter as that's what most everything has and you won't need a usb to usb-C dongle like the Babyface. M62 has remote control ... so it sort of can work as a replacement ADI-2 Pro FS R BE in some ways actually if you just plug some speakers into the HP out. The DX1 II definitely replaced the ADI 2/4 I had for sure though: 10 band EQ + digital out + remote + HP is honestly amazing.
I use the DX1 II as main output: digital out to Optical to SPDIF converter with a SPDIF to AES/EBU passive adaptor + headphone output. One of the few things that actually has digital volume control for the output. I just plug this M62 in whenever I need to calibrate my speakers with DIRAC (headphone out to speakers works fine) or record something. I do wish this had digital output with volume control or even a second analog out so I don't have to choose between headphones or speakers though.
I'd hope their future larger desktop devices incorporate the preamps these have + a Guitar DI (All digitally stepped so can match levels) or THAT 1580/other padless preamps with a bit more headroom for hotter mics would be nicer though - or these pres + a optional pad for hot signals. I'd love it if they kept using remote controls for desktop models too. I'll also throw out Digital outputs and volume control of them so you can have digital and analog speakers all controlled by a remote/knob would be great for surround mixing. The DX1 II having volume-controllable digital outputs is a step in the right direction!
